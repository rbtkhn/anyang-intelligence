Why it matters:

The episode treats an installed skill as a transfer of someone else's operational judgment, acceptable shortcuts, tools, and definition of done—not as a neutral app installation. Its strongest value is a governed skill lifecycle: establish purpose and provenance, keep activation metadata precise, preserve human inspectability, customize with visible lineage, and audit the portfolio for conflicts and bloat.

Dominant seams:
- Skill provenance and capability transfer should be reviewed before installation or adoption.
- Skills need a dual-audience, progressive-disclosure contract: agents must discover and invoke them reliably while humans can inspect their judgment.
- Tacit operator judgment can become a repeatable skill, but customization should preserve lineage and explicit changes.
- Growing skill portfolios need read-only conflict, duplication, activation, and context-burden audits before additive fixes.
- Adoption percentages, product behavior, security generalizations, and promotional claims remain source-attributed and provisional.

Best candidate primitive:

A skill adoption and portfolio-integrity gate: state the intended job, inspect provenance and authority, verify activation metadata and progressive disclosure, record lineage and modifications, test representative use, and audit interactions before adding another skill.

Likely best receiving lane:

Internal operating-substrate skill governance. Any customer-lane adaptation would require separate translation, security review, and lane-owner approval.

Usable claims:
- Installing a skill imports operational assumptions and judgment that should be inspectable.
- Discovery metadata, the main instruction file, and supporting resources serve different loading roles.
- Human readability and agent usability are joint design requirements rather than substitutes.
- Additive skill growth can create duplication, conflict, and context burden that warrant an audit before another installation.
- Skill lineage helps distinguish inherited mechanisms from local modifications.
