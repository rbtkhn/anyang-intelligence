# Artistic Director Phase 1 — Calibration Exercise

**Purpose:** Learn how to work with an AI as a creative collaborator.  
**Time:** 60–90 minutes  
**Materials:** Your OB1 repository, one AI tool, and your own judgment  
**Do not use:** live projects, private information, or protected material

## The exercise

Create three different creative directions for a fictional cultural event.

Use this starting brief:

> A small evening event called **Afterglow** invites people to slow down,
> notice their surroundings, and share ideas with strangers. Create three
> possible visual and verbal directions for the event.

You may change the event name, audience, setting, or mood if another subject
helps you think more clearly. Keep the subject fictional and simple.

## Step 1 — Ask the AI for options

Give the AI this prompt, adapting it to your own voice:

```text
I am learning how to work with you as a creative collaborator.

Create three materially different creative directions for this fictional event:
[describe the event in one or two sentences].

For each direction, give me:
- a short concept name;
- the central idea;
- the intended audience feeling;
- a visual language;
- a color or material mood;
- a typography or lettering direction;
- a short verbal line or tagline; and
- the main risk or weakness of the direction.

Do not make the three directions minor variations of one idea. Make their
underlying choices genuinely different. Explain your reasoning briefly.
```

Save the prompt and the AI's response in `artistic-director/first-exercise.md`.

## Step 2 — Make your own judgment

Read the three directions before asking the AI what it thinks.

Write short answers:

- Which direction is strongest?
- Which direction is most original?
- Which direction would be easiest to recognize visually?
- Which direction feels wrong or generic?
- What did the AI understand well?
- What did it misunderstand?

Do not judge only by whether you like the wording. Look for the quality of the
underlying idea and whether the visual and verbal choices belong together.

## Step 3 — Critique one direction

Write a short critique using these headings:

```text
What works:
What does not work:
What feels generic:
What I would preserve:
What I would change:
What the audience should feel instead:
```

Be specific. “I do not like it” is a reaction. “The soft colors and slow
language make the event feel passive, but I want it to feel quietly magnetic”
is useful training information.

## Step 4 — Ask for a revision

Send the AI your critique:

```text
Here is my critique of Direction [name]:

[paste your critique]

Revise the direction using my critique. Keep [the thing you want preserved].
Change [the thing you want changed]. Explain what you changed and why. If my
critique is ambiguous, ask me one question before revising.
```

Save the critique, the AI's revision, and its explanation in
`artistic-director/first-exercise.md`.

## Step 5 — Record three lessons

In `artistic-director/lessons.md`, record:

1. **Keep:** one behavior or creative principle you want the AI to repeat.
2. **Avoid:** one behavior or creative choice you want the AI to avoid.
3. **Still unclear:** one thing the AI does not yet understand about your taste
   or judgment.

Use this format:

```text
Keep:
Evidence:

Avoid:
Evidence:

Still unclear:
What I will test next:
```

Do not turn every preference into a permanent rule. A lesson may be accepted,
rejected, or held for further testing.

## Completion evidence

The exercise is complete when `first-exercise.md` contains:

- the starting brief;
- the prompt used;
- three distinct AI directions;
- your comparative judgment;
- your critique of one direction;
- the AI's revision; and
- the AI's explanation of its revision.

And `lessons.md` contains one Keep, one Avoid, and one Still unclear lesson.

The goal is not a finished brand or beautiful final artifact. The goal is to
make the human's judgment visible enough that the AI can begin learning it.
