# Nate Herk Analysis: Evidence Before Authority in AI Delivery

## Decision Compression

- What changed: The twelve lessons resolve into one delivery loop connecting business constraint, explicit outcome, encoded expertise, bounded capability, and repeated verification.
- Reusable mechanism: Use an evidence-before-authority gate that couples a North Star and outcome receipt with representative evaluations, minimum tool permissions, and human approval.
- Decision implication: Compare this source-derived gate with existing repository controls before testing any missing question in one approved internal workflow.
- Evidence still missing: Independent corroboration, controlled workflow results, evaluation-set quality, exception rates, review burden, cost data, and verification of source-specific incidents and benchmarks.
- Recommended disposition: lane-test-ready

## Lead Judgment

The strongest reusable mechanism is not “use AI first” but “earn authority with evidence”: start from an operational constraint, state the measurable finish line, give the system only the capability it needs, and require proof across representative cases. Claims about commercial success, incident scale, completion percentages, cost reductions, model leadership, and career outcomes remain provisional.

## Controlling Object

Whether AI-enabled work is sufficiently outcome-bound, capability-constrained, and repeatedly verified to justify real operational authority.

## Seam Analysis

### Seam: outcome receipt and North Star

- Core claim: Begin from the actual business constraint, choose one target metric, and document the baseline-to-result change.
- Likely durable: A predeclared outcome and baseline make value claims more reviewable and reduce solution-first building.
- Likely speculative: That a small number of receipts reliably proves general competence or causes role and customer outcomes.
- Verification burden: Confirm baseline integrity, attribution, time horizon, confounders, and whether the result persists.
- Primitive opportunity: Constraint-to-outcome receipt linking owner, baseline, target, evidence, exceptions, and review date.
- Lane-safe routing opportunity: Ask what measurable constraint the work changes and what evidence would distinguish success from a persuasive demo.
- Membrane risk: A selected metric can be gamed or can conceal quality, safety, and distributional harms.
- ROI disposition: lane-test-ready

### Seam: managed orchestration and finish line

- Lane-safe routing opportunity: Ask whether the system has enough evidence to act, what done means, and what delegated work remains reviewable.
- Membrane risk: Management metaphors may imply employment-like discretion or authorize subagents beyond the operator's actual authority.

### Seam: verification, evals, and capability boundaries

- Core claim: A successful run is weak evidence; consequential systems need representative repeated evaluation, explicit review criteria, and tool-level least privilege.
- Likely durable: Capability restrictions are stronger than prompt-only prohibitions, and repeated testing is more informative than a single demonstration.
- Likely speculative: The reported incident scale, implied completion percentages, and whether self-verification alone can establish confidence.
- Verification burden: High; validate evaluation-set provenance, scoring validity, tool enforcement, adversarial cases, exception handling, and independent human review.
- Primitive opportunity: Evidence-before-authority gate combining permission inventory, representative cases, scoring method, exception log, and named approver.
- Lane-safe routing opportunity: None beyond repository comparison until the proposed gate is checked against existing controls and tested.
- Membrane risk: LLM-as-judge and self-verification can create circular assurance; permission scoping can still miss side effects or indirect tools.
- ROI disposition: primitive-candidate

### Seam: cost-aware model routing

- Core claim: Match each task to the least expensive model that can meet the required quality threshold.
- Likely durable: Model choice should be task- and evidence-dependent rather than uniformly maximal.
- Likely speculative: Named-model comparisons, “same quality” assertions, tenfold savings, and claims about local-model trajectories.
- Verification burden: Measure quality, latency, total cost, retry rate, exception rate, privacy, and operational complexity on representative work.
- Primitive opportunity: Model-routing evaluation matrix, but only after a controlled comparison establishes thresholds.
- Lane-safe routing opportunity: None until verified with current models and actual lane workloads.
- Membrane risk: Cost optimization may quietly reduce quality, safety, privacy, or reliability.
- ROI disposition: needs-verification
