# Game Design

> **Status:** Provisional internal capability lane. No game, customer,
> production commitment, budget, or external claim is created by this charter.

Game Design is an internal Anyang Intelligence capability for designing
interactive systems, testing meaningful player choices through lightweight
prototypes, and converting play evidence into clearer mechanics, experiences,
and implementation briefs.

## Purpose

Game Design exists to answer bounded questions about how a game should feel,
what choices it should create, and whether its rules produce the intended
experience. It begins with design evidence rather than production scope.

The lane may prepare:

- player promises and design pillars;
- mechanics, rules, feedback, and failure states;
- core gameplay loops;
- progression and reward structures;
- narrative-system interactions;
- paper and low-fidelity prototypes;
- playtest questions, observations, and decision notes;
- accessibility, safety, balance, monetization, and manipulation-risk reviews;
- implementation-ready design briefs.

## Initial State

- Named game: none.
- Customer or external partner: none.
- Budget and spend authority: `$0`.
- Production capacity: unassigned.
- Engine, platform, genre, and audience: undecided.
- Publication or distribution authority: none.
- First proof: one bounded design question and the smallest prototype capable
  of testing it.

The lane remains inactive until an exact game-design question, decision owner,
evidence boundary, and next approval gate are named.

## Operating Loop

```text
player need or experience hypothesis
  -> design pillars
  -> materially different mechanic alternatives
  -> smallest playable prototype
  -> bounded internal playtest
  -> observed behavior and feedback
  -> revise / continue / hold
  -> implementation-ready design brief
```

## Core Memory Objects

- game concept;
- player promise;
- design pillars;
- mechanic hypothesis and rule set;
- core loop and progression model;
- prototype;
- playtest question, session, and observation;
- design decision and rationale;
- balance, accessibility, safety, rights, and manipulation risks;
- implementation brief.

## Capability Boundaries

Game Design does not, by implication:

- produce artwork, video, audio, marketing, or promotional assets;
- select software architecture or begin production implementation;
- publish, distribute, spend, hire, purchase, or make external commitments;
- use licensed intellectual property or unclear-rights source material;
- collect player data;
- approve gambling-like systems or manipulative monetization;
- create child-facing experiences or educational claims;
- transfer customer, family, learner, or other lane-specific facts.

Those actions require their own scope, authority, evidence, privacy, rights,
and safety review.

## Adjacent Lanes

### Media Production

Media Production may create approved art, narrative, audio, trailers, and
campaign assets. It does not silently determine game mechanics or the intended
player experience.

### System Engineering

System Engineering may implement approved systems and technical prototypes. It
does not silently choose design pillars, player-facing rules, or product scope.

### Singularity Science

Singularity Science may supply translated research about AI agents,
simulation, cognition, and interactive systems. Source bulk and untested claims
do not become Game Design doctrine.

### Learning Core

Only generic play, motivation, or interaction primitives may be considered for
Learning Core, and only after strict child-safety, parent-authority,
privacy, and educational-evidence review. Learner or family facts do not cross
into Game Design.

## Membrane Rule

Transfer primitives, not private context. Before accepting an insight from
another lane, identify its source, consent basis, evidence, sensitivity,
authority owner, and safe translated form. The strictest applicable membrane
controls.

## First Activation Gate

Before beginning design work, record:

1. the exact player or experience question;
2. the decision owner;
3. the intended audience and safety classification;
4. permitted source material and rights basis;
5. the smallest prototype and playtest boundary;
6. evidence required to continue, revise, or hold;
7. implementation, publication, spending, and data actions that remain
   separately unauthorized.

Until this gate is complete, Game Design is a bounded capability charter rather
than an active game project.
