# Artistic Director–AI Factory Operating Contract

Status: `prepared — internal contract; Artistic Director activation and task approval still required`

## Purpose

Define a batch-oriented creative studio in which the Artistic Director owns creative thesis and selection while an AI factory prepares production materials and reusable creative assets.

The first objective is distinctive, alive creative work and reusable creative infrastructure. Throughput, finished episodes, platform metrics, and revenue claims are secondary until the creative system has passed review.

## Operating loop

```text
shared studio review
  -> creative thesis
  -> bounded batch brief
  -> asset / canon preparation
  -> AI production packet
  -> Artistic Director selection
  -> quality / rights review
  -> canon and decision-log update
```

## Role contract

### Artistic Director

Owns:

- creative thesis, taste, and intended viewer effect;
- acceptance and rejection of directions and references;
- what becomes canon or remains project-specific;
- creative decision receipts and rejection reasoning;
- judgment about whether work is distinctive, alive, generic, derivative, or strategically empty.

Does not receive automatic authority over publication, client commitments, spend, rights clearance, contractor engagement, or external communication.

### Executive Assistant

Supports the shared studio cadence by:

- preparing briefs and review packets;
- organizing references, versions, and asset metadata;
- preserving evidence and decision receipts;
- tracking follow-up, unresolved questions, and stop conditions;
- keeping creative judgment attributable to the Artistic Director.

The Executive Assistant does not convert preparation into approval or execution authority.

### AI factory

May prepare:

- style-bible drafts;
- character, location, and voice reference variants;
- scene and shot breakdowns;
- prompt packets;
- continuity checks;
- missing-asset alerts;
- accepted/rejected reference comparisons;
- reusable asset metadata;
- review-ready creative packets.

## v1 boundary: prepare-only

The factory may not autonomously:

- render or consume paid production credits;
- schedule recurring production execution;
- publish or distribute;
- contact clients, customers, contractors, or audiences;
- spend money or commit budget;
- clear rights, likeness, claims, or attribution;
- promote an asset into canon;
- imply approval, delivery, or publication readiness.

All such actions return to the applicable human and Media Production gates.

## Memory structure

Use two linked but separate memory surfaces.

### Authority and source-of-truth hierarchy

- Repository contracts, gates, and approved briefs define doctrine and the
  permitted operating boundary.
- The private Executive Council ledger remains canonical for live authority,
  execution, evidence, and outcome state.
- Canon, decision-log, dashboard, review, and compounding records prepared
  under this contract are supporting creative-memory objects. They may link to
  Council transactions but may not replace, override, or grant them.
- Dashboard and schema-valid records are derived views until their referenced
  authority and evidence resolve through the governing source.
- When a supporting record conflicts with a governing gate, authority receipt,
  or Council transaction, choose `hold` and reconcile the governing source.

### Canon

Stores accepted, reusable creative rules and references:

- style rules;
- approved character, location, voice, and asset references;
- reusable shot grammar;
- current creative constraints.

Every canon entry records version, provenance, owner, review status, reuse boundary, and related decision receipt.

### Decision log

Stores dated judgment:

- accepted and rejected directions;
- reasons for rejection;
- unresolved creative, rights, and authority questions;
- evidence used;
- what is safe to reuse and what must remain project-specific.

Creative canon may be updated only after an attributable Artistic Director decision and the applicable review gate. A generated asset is never canon merely because it is technically consistent.

## Review standard

Review the following separately:

1. Creative vitality and emotional effect.
2. Visual continuity and technical polish.
3. Source, rights, claim, likeness, and representation discipline.
4. Reuse potential and asset-library value.
5. Audience, client, or lane suitability.

Continuity is a guardrail, not the definition of quality. A consistent asset may still be rejected as generic, lifeless, derivative, or strategically empty.

## Required batch packet

Every batch prepared under this contract contains:

- task ID and creative thesis;
- lane and source boundary;
- audience or viewer effect;
- do-not-invent and do-not-copy list;
- style, character, location, voice, and asset references;
- scene or shot plan;
- expected output and disposition: `explore / shortlist / brief / hold / reject`;
- rights, claims, privacy, likeness, and representation risks;
- capacity and budget status;
- reviewer and expiration/stop condition;
- proposed canon and decision-log updates.

## First bounded exercise

Prepare one internal, non-client-facing batch containing:

- one creative thesis;
- one style-bible draft;
- two character references;
- two location references;
- one voice direction;
- three scene or shot concepts;
- one proposed accepted direction;
- one proposed rejected direction with reason.

The packet must produce a canon update candidate, accepted/rejected reference log entries, one Artistic Director creative decision receipt, one Creative Abundance Ledger entry, and an unresolved rights/authority list.

The exercise stops before rendering, publication, client delivery, spend, or external communication.

## Acceptance criteria

The batch passes internal review only when:

- the Artistic Director's creative judgment is visible and attributable;
- the factory reduces preparation burden without flattening distinction;
- accepted and rejected learning are separate;
- canon and decisions are independently reviewable;
- assets are portable and versioned;
- rights and authority gaps are explicit;
- no automated step implies approval or publication;
- at least one reusable creative asset or rule is worth retaining.
